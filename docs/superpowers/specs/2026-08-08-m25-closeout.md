## The result of the probe

**Verdict: `StoredSession.host`/`port`/`username`/`authKind` stay — but for
a different reason than in M24.**

### Method

`@available(*, deprecated, message: "M25 probe — every reader must be
SSH-guarded")` on all four declarations, then `rm -rf .build && swift build
--build-tests`. The **clean** rebuild is essential: an incremental run does
not recompile every file and can swallow warnings.
`--build-tests` is needed because the four accessors have been `internal`
since M23 — `MacSCPApp` and `MacSCPCLI` cannot see them at all, the probe
surface is `macSCPCore` plus its `@testable` test target. That no App or
CLI file appears in the list below is therefore not a finding but a
consequence of visibility.

No `grep`: M24 measured that a grep for `.host`/`.port` returns 241 hits,
the large majority of which are `URL.host`, `StoredSSHConfig.host`,
`JumpSpec.host` and foreign types.

### The deletion attempt and what it actually showed

The brief specifies: if all readers are guarded, delete the four
declarations together with `@available` and rebuild; if something breaks,
a reader was not guarded after all. The attempt was carried out:

```
swift build --build-tests
→ 17 error lines at 15 production sites in 3 files
  (6× SessionListViewModel, 7× LoginResolver, 4× SSHFieldSchema;
   2 of the 17 are follow-on errors from type inference at :264)
```

The library fails to build, so the test target does not even get compiled
— the 38 test sites do not even come into play. The change was reverted
(`git checkout --`, working tree clean again).

**The brief's closing rule does not hold here, and that is the real
yield of the probe.** The break does *not* prove that a reader was
unguarded. Being guarded and naming the accessor are independent: a
reader behind `kind == .ssh` names the accessor exactly as an unguarded
one does. A deletion is only free once there are **zero** readers — not
once all of them are guarded. The spec silently equated the two notions.

### Why the accessors stay

M24 kept them because five readers were unguarded. That reason is
**gone**. The new one is:

A deletion would not be cleanup, but duplication. The fallback expression
(`ssh?.host ?? ""`, `ssh?.port ?? 22`, …) does not vanish, it moves from
one declaration into 15 production and 38 test sites. Eleven of the 15
production sites sit behind `kind` guards the compiler does not see —
each would have to spell out the fallback again or force-unwrap. And
`StoredSessionTests.swift:76–77` would lose its subject.

What would turn the deletion into cleanup is not a deletion, but a
decision M25 did not make: giving the guarded readers a
**non-optional** SSH block (`guard let ssh = referenced.ssh`), so that
`SSHFieldSchema.values(from:)` remains the only place that writes the
fallback at all. That is a behavior decision for the blockless `.ssh`
session — it belongs in its own spec with its own test, not in a
completion report.

**Recommended next move:** ask exactly that question — what should
happen to an `.ssh` session without a block? Once that is answered, the
deletion is mechanical.

## What the process produced

**The lesson of this milestone is an addendum to the M24 lesson ("where a
question is 'which sites does this affect?', the compiler is a better
witness than a grep"):**

> The compiler answers **which sites**, not **whether they are fine**. A
> probe whose abort condition is "does the build break?" confuses
> *naming* a symbol with *misusing* it. Whoever wants to delete a
> declaration must count how many sites **name** it — not how many
> **misuse** it. Both numbers were different here:
> 0 misuses, 53 mentions.

Second, concrete and cheap: an `@available` probe belongs on a **clean**
tree (`rm -rf .build`). The first, incremental run of this session
reported 118 lines of warnings from compiler context excerpts and
yielded no cleanly countable result; only the rebuild with a filter on
`^/Users/.../:` gave the exact count of 53.

Third, on the relationship between the brief and reality: the brief gave
a decision rule for step 3 that does not logically hold. It was
executed (the deletion attempt was run, the result is above), but its
conclusion was **not** adopted. A brief is a hypothesis about the
approach, the same way a file list is a hypothesis about the scope.

**Fourth (addition of the whole-branch review), the generalization of
point three:** a probe needs its abort condition phrased in the terms
of the tool it actually uses — not in the terms of the question it is
meant to answer. `@available(*, deprecated)` plus `swift build`
measures **naming** (does any still-compiling caller call the
declaration by name?). But the brief wrote its abort rule in terms of
**correctness** — "are all readers guarded? then delete" — and naming and
correctness are independent properties: a reader guarded by `kind ==
.ssh` names the accessor exactly as an unguarded one does, as the
deletion attempt above showed. That is the same class of error as the
grep-vs-compiler lesson of the previous milestone, just one level up:
there the wrong tool (grep) measured the right question imprecisely;
here the right tool (the compiler) measured a different question than
the brief thought it was asking.
